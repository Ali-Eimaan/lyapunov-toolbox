\begin{abstract}
\texttt{verify\_sublevel\_set} solves an SOS program that certifies $\dot{V} \le 0$
on $\{V \le \rho\}$. The condition is \emph{sufficient}, not necessary —
solvers may miss a certificate. Putinar's Positivstellensatz tells us when
the search space is complete (no missed certificates), and every multiplier in
the program carries its own SOS certificate, making the whole proof
self-contained and machine-checkable.
\end{abstract}

\section{From Lyapunov to semialgebraic set}

The Lyapunov condition
\begin{equation}
  \dot{V}(x) \le -\varepsilon\|x - x_{\mathrm{eq}}\|^{2k}
  \quad \text{on} \quad
  \mathcal{K} = \{\, x \mid V(x) \le \rho \,\}
\end{equation}
is a \emph{non-negativity constraint on a semialgebraic set}. $\mathcal{K}$
is defined by the single polynomial inequality $p_1(x) := \rho - V(x) \ge 0$,
and globally non-negative polynomials are the \emph{easiest} certificates
(just SOS), but constrained non-negativity is harder.

\section{The (generalised) S-procedure}

Let $q(x) := -\dot{V}(x) - \varepsilon\|x - x_{\mathrm{eq}}\|^{2k}$. The
classical S-procedure says: if there exists $\lambda \in \R_{\ge 0}$ such
that $q(x) - \lambda(\rho - V(x))$ is SOS, then $q \ge 0$ on $\{V \le \rho\}$.

The generalisation used in this toolbox lifts $\lambda$ to a \emph{polynomial}
multiplier, and adds SOS multipliers $\sigma_j$ for semialgebraic constraints
$g_j(x) \ge 0$ and free-sign polynomial multipliers $\mu_k$ for equality
constraints $h_k(x) = 0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:putinar}
  q(x) + \lambda(x)(V(x) - \rho)
  - \sum_{j} \sigma_j(x) g_j(x)
  - \sum_{k} \mu_k(x) h_k(x)
  \in \Sigma_{n,2d}.
\end{equation}

Each $\sigma_j$ is SOS (its own Gram matrix), $\lambda$ is SOS, and each
$\mu_k$ is an unrestricted polynomial (linear in its monomial coefficients).
The multipliers are \emph{unknowns} of the SDP, found by the solver.

\section{Putinar's Positivstellensatz}

The representation~\eqref{eq:putinar} is sufficient but not necessary.
Putinar (1993) proved that under the \emph{Archimedean condition} —
existence of a polynomial $N - \|x\|^2$ in the quadratic module generated
by $\{g_j\}$ — every polynomial \emph{strictly} positive on $\mathcal{K}$
admits such a representation. This means:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{If the Archimedean condition holds:} infeasibility of
    the SDP means $q$ genuinely fails to be positive on $\mathcal{K}$
    (no certificate exists at \emph{any} degree).
  \item \textbf{If it does not hold:} infeasibility means no certificate
    exists \emph{at this degree}, and the user may increase the degree
    or add a redundant ball constraint.
\end{itemize}

The \texttt{unicycle\_with\_obstacle} example manufactures the Archimedean
condition by adding $g_{\mathrm{ball}}(x) = N - \|x\|^2 \ge 0$ with
large $N$, so the SDP is provably complete.

\section{Equality constraints and free-sign multipliers}

The invariant $s^2 + c^2 = 1$ in the pendulum/exact and unicycle examples
is an \emph{equality} constraint. The multiplier $\mu(x)$ is an unrestricted
polynomial (coefficients unconstrained), not SOS. The term $-\mu(x)h(x)$
in~\eqref{eq:putinar} does not affect the sign condition on the manifold
$h(x) = 0$ but provides degrees of freedom that can help satisfy the SOS
condition off the manifold — a crucial mechanism for manifold-constrained
systems.

\section{The witness: Gram matrices for every multiplier}

Every multiplier ($\lambda$, each $\sigma_j$, and the SOS certificate
for $V - \varepsilon\|\Delta x\|^{2k}$) carries a Gram pair
\texttt{(basis, Q)} with $Q \succeq 0$. This is what makes the certificate
self-contained:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{verify\_certificate(cert, sys)} reconstructs the \emph{target}
    polynomial for each Gram pair from the certificate's own data.
  \item For $\lambda$: the target is $\lambda$ itself (the multiplier
    is the certificate that it is SOS).
  \item For the derivative: the target is
    $-\dot{V} - \varepsilon\|\Delta x\|^{2k} + \lambda(V - \rho)
     - \sum \sigma_j g_j - \sum \mu_k h_k$.
  \item \texttt{gram\_residual(target, basis, Q)} checks that the
    Gram matrix reproduces its target polynomial, not just that $Q$ is PSD.
\end{enumerate}

A Gram matrix that is PSD but paired with the \emph{wrong} basis monomials
(or that reconstructs a different polynomial from the target) is detected
and rejected by the residual check.

\section{The obstacle constraint: example}

For the unicycle with obstacle, $g_{\mathrm{obs}}(x) = (x - x_o)^2 +
(y - y_o)^2 - r^2 \ge 0$. The S-procedure term is
$-\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}(x) g_{\mathrm{obs}}(x)$, which adds a positive
penalty when $g_{\mathrm{obs}} < 0$ (inside the obstacle), making the
derivative constraint \emph{harder} to satisfy there. The certified set
is thus provably obstacle-free: the certificate holds only on
$\{g_{\mathrm{obs}} \ge 0\}$.

\section{Relation to SOS decomposition}

The companion document \texttt{sos\_decomposition.tex} covers the Gram matrix
parameterisation that makes these programs SDPs. The key insight of
Putinar's Positivstellensatz is that the same Gram matrix machinery extends
from global positivity to constrained positivity: the multipliers absorb
the constraints, turning a local condition into a \emph{global} SOS check.
